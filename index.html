Price sensitivity (Modern Van Westendorp)

The four price points form a "baseball diamond": PMC (third base) and PME (first base) bound the acceptable range, OPP (home plate) is the single best price, and IPP (second base) is the price customers expect. The meter is a blunt tool: it ignores competitors' prices and respondents can game it.

Demand forecast: Q = N × A × P

N – annual purchases in your market

Students at target campuses, factories in a region, annual units sold in the category…

Durable goods are often below 1 (0.33 = replaced every 3 years).

Top-down: start from a published total (enrollment, census, industry sales) and narrow it. Example: ~1,000,000 off-campus students living 1–3 miles from the 100 largest campuses. Bottom-up: count buyers in segments you can name, e.g. 150,000 bicycles and scooters sold to factories per year. Cite sources.

A – awareness × availability

Share of the market that will hear about the product in year one.

Share of aware buyers who can actually get it through your channels.

A typically ranges from about 0.2 (realistic early penetration) to 0.8 (sold everywhere, e.g. every bike shop). A single distributor's market share (e.g. 0.30) is a reasonable proxy. Be skeptical of A above 0.5 in year one.
